Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 31, 2026

This policy explains what information is handled when you use the Pulse API load tester at apulse.tech.

API tests run from your browser

When you start a test, your browser sends requests directly to the endpoint you enter. Those requests may include the URL, method, headers, authentication token, and request body you configure. The endpoint you choose receives the requests and may process or log them under its own privacy practices. Only test endpoints you are authorized to use, and avoid entering secrets unless you understand the destination and its logging practices.

Pulse does not operate a proxy for these tests. Test settings, live metrics, response previews, and logs are held in your browser for the current page session and are not sent to a Pulse application server by the testing code. They are cleared when you clear the results or close/reload the page. Your browser may retain ordinary network or cache data according to its settings.

Information stored in your browser

Pulse uses browser local storage to remember your light or dark theme preference. Google Analytics may use first-party cookies to estimate unique visitors and sessions. Test configuration and results are kept in page memory for the current session. Pulse does not create user accounts or save test history.

Hosting and basic technical data

The site is delivered through Cloudflare. When you visit it, Cloudflare processes network and request information needed to deliver and protect the site, such as your IP address, requested URL, and technical connection details. Cloudflare also uses a browser analytics beacon for page-view and performance measurements. Cloudflare may process this information under its own service terms and privacy policy. The LaunchBuff badge on the homepage loads from launchbuff.com, which receives the connection details needed to serve its image. Pulse does not use third-party advertising scripts.

Website analytics

Pulse uses Google Analytics 4 to measure page views and estimate daily visitors. Google's tag receives connection details, including your IP address, and may collect the page title and path, referrer page, browser and device information, and approximate location. It may set cookies to distinguish users and sessions. Pulse removes query strings and URL fragments from the page and referrer URLs it supplies to Analytics. When a valid API test starts, Pulse sends an api_test_started event without the endpoint, credentials, request details, or results. Google processes analytics data under its own privacy practices. You can limit this measurement using your browser's cookie and tracking controls.

Exports

If you choose CSV or JSON export, the file is generated in your browser and downloaded to your device. It can contain endpoint URLs, response headers, and response previews. Keep exported files secure and review them before sharing.

The rate-limit analyzer also offers a separate aggregate report. Its download and share link omit endpoint URLs, credentials, request and response headers, and response bodies. The share link places the aggregate report in the URL fragment, which can remain in your browser history and can be read by anyone you send the link to. Pulse does not upload or store these reports.

Children

Pulse is a general-purpose developer tool and is not designed for children to use independently. Do not submit a child's personal information through the service.

Changes to this policy
